10 · Synthesis

Comparative Verdict

Across 11 parameters, each lake presents a distinct and serious pollution profile. Neither is clean — but the nature and severity of their impairment differs fundamentally.

Hesaraghatta Lake
🔴pH crisis: Exceeds BIS upper limit (8.5) at every sample point. Avg 9.34 — severe alkalinity stress on aquatic ecology.
🔴Biological contamination: 1,450 CFU/100 mL — 11× Varthur, indicating systemic fecal inputs from sewage or agricultural runoff.
🟡Oxygen stress: Avg DO of 4.34 mg/L. Three of five samples dip below the BIS Class D fisheries threshold of 4 mg/L.
🟡Hardness spike: Sample 5 at 370 mg/L exceeds the IS 10500 limit of 300 mg/L — significant intra-lake variation noted.
🟡Nitrate present: 15 mg/L — within BIS limits but indicates inorganic nitrogen inputs, possibly from agricultural drainage or decomposition.
✅Advantages: Lower turbidity (5.46 vs 9.00 NTU), lower TDS (178.6 vs 369.9 ppm), lower COD (32 vs 71 mg/L), and lower phosphorus (0.04 vs 0.22 mg/L).
Varthur Lake
🔴Turbidity: Avg 9.00 NTU — consistently above WHO 5 NTU limit. S1 at 12.3 NTU is the highest single reading across both lakes.
🔴Phosphorus excess: 0.22 mg/L — 5.5× Hesaraghatta's level, exceeding the eutrophication threshold and posing a long-term algal bloom risk.
🔴COD load: 71 mg/L — 2.2× Hesaraghatta's COD. Indicates substantially higher dissolved organic matter from urban and sewage inputs.
🟡Dissolved solids: Avg TDS of 369.938 ppm — more than 2× Hesaraghatta's. Both within IS limit, but Varthur's sustained load reflects persistent ion inputs.
🟡Coliform: 132 CFU/100 mL — far below Hesaraghatta, but still 132× above WHO standards and biologically unsafe without treatment.
✅Advantages: Better dissolved oxygen (6.24 vs 4.34 mg/L), pH within BIS range (8.08), and lower coliform, hardness, nitrate, and ammonia.
🔬 Overall assessment: Hesaraghatta is more severely affected by biological and alkaline contamination — its pH exceedance, coliform count, and DO deficiency pose acute ecological and public health risks. Varthur shows a more chemically and organically polluted profile — high turbidity, TDS, COD, and phosphorus reflect the cumulative impact of urban sewage and runoff. Both lakes are unfit for drinking, direct contact, or fisheries support without significant remediation. Targeted interventions differ: Hesaraghatta requires sewage source control and alkalinity management; Varthur requires nutrient load reduction, turbidity control, and organic waste interception.

Data source: Field measurements from Hesaraghatta Lake and Varthur Lake, Bengaluru · 5 samples per lake · BIS IS 2296:1992 & IS 10500 standards · WHO Drinking Water Guidelines · This analysis presents measured values only — no values have been fabricated or extrapolated.
